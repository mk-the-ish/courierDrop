1.0 Introduction 
1.1 Problem Statement 
In urban areas like Harare, there is an underutilized network of informal transporters making regular trips. Concurrently, clients struggle to find affordable and reliable package delivery options. The lack of a digital platform to connect these groups leads to wasted vehicle space and missed economic opportunities. The core problem is the lack of a mechanism to match delivery demand with pre-existing, informal transport supply. We need a platform to algorithmically identify delivery opportunities along a transporter’s planned route, creating an efficient, low-cost logistics solution. 
1.2 Aim 
This project aims to design, develop, and evaluate a comprehensive mobile application and web-based platform that creates a peer-to-peer logistics marketplace. The focus is on enhancing efficiency and reducing a city’s carbon footprint by leveraging existing trips for delivery purposes. 
1.3 Research Objectives 
1. To conduct a comprehensive analysis of the informal transport and delivery sector, identifying key operational patterns and criteria for service exchange. 
2. To design and implement a novel route-matching algorithm to intelligently connect requests with planned routes. 
3. To develop a cross-platform mobile application, designed with a connectivity-aware architecture to ensure reliable tracking and communication in environments with intermittent internet access. 
4. To create a robust, cloud-based backend and database for managing users, real-time trip data, and transactions. 
5. To evaluate the system’s performance in a pilot study using a unique set of metrics, including average match rate and user satisfaction scores.
1.4 Scope and Limitations 
The project focuses on intra-city deliveries within a specific urban area (e.g., Harare). Deliverables include a functional mobile app for clients and transporters and a web-based dashboard. Limitations include the initial lack of a large user base to ensure a high match rate, and the exclusion of specialized deliveries (e.g., temperature-controlled, large-scale freight). 
1.5 Feasibility Study 
The project is technically feasible using widely-used mobile development frameworks (e.g., React Native) and robust cloud services (e.g., Firestore). Economically, it is viable by relying on open-source software and a subscription-based cloud model. Operationally, it addresses a significant market need, ensuring high potential for user adoption. 
1.6 Work Plan 
The project will be executed in three major phases over a 14-week timeline: 
1. Phase 1: Planning and Design (Weeks 1-4): Market research, requirements analysis, system architecture design, UI/UX wireframes. 
2. Phase 2: Development and Implementation (Weeks 5-10): Building the mobile app, backend services, and implementing core algorithms. 
3. Phase 3: Evaluation and Refinement (Weeks 11-14): Pilot testing, user feedback collection, performance improvements, and documentation finalization. 
1.7 Project Budget 
The budget is primarily allocated to cloud services to ensure scalability and reliability.

1.8 Literature Review 
The proposed DropCity platform is an intelligent peer-to-peer (P2P) logistics system leveraging modern mobile architecture and advanced routing algorithms. The foundation of this project rests on three pillars of recent academic and industry research: Crowdsourced Delivery Optimization, Route-Alignment, and Connectivity-Aware Data Synchronization.
1.8.1 Pre-existing Solutions and Market Gaps
The crowdsourced logistics market is populated by various models, ranging from enterprise delivery networks (e.g., dedicated fleets, food delivery) to true peer-to-peer (P2P) platforms.
Pre-existing Solutions:
Ride-Hailing & Bid-Based Services (e.g., inDrive, Lalamove, Uber Connect): These platforms allow users to request delivery and often involve a bid or fixed-rate model. Their primary mechanism is typically proximity-based matching—connecting a user to the nearest available driver. The driver often makes a dedicated trip for the parcel, which results in higher costs and lower environmental efficiency compared to integrating a delivery into an existing, necessary journey.
Specialized P2P Networks (e.g., Roadie, CitizenShipper): These focus largely on long-haul or non-standard items (furniture, pets). While they are P2P, their matching criteria are still often optimized for making a trip, not for integrating a parcel into a commute.
The DropCity Gap:
The fundamental gap DropCity addresses is the failure of existing platforms to prioritize the efficiency of the Courier's pre-planned, existing route. Current P2P models typically introduce a new detour, treating the delivery as a dedicated task. DropCity is uniquely positioned by its focus on:
Route-Alignment: Finding a nearby driver, DropCity filters for couriers whose existing route already aligns with the parcel's trajectory (Section 2.3). .
Connectivity Resilience: As detailed below, DropCity’s Connectivity-Aware Architecture provides critical data integrity in areas with poor cellular service, a common logistics challenge that most consumer-grade P2P apps do not robustly address.


1.8.2 Crowdsourced Delivery and Routing Efficiency
Crowdsourced delivery (CD) has rapidly evolved as a viable solution to the complexities of urban last-mile logistics, heavily relying on mobile platforms and dynamic algorithmic matching (Wang, 2024; EasyRoutes, 2025). Research confirms that CD services gain efficiency through algorithmic optimization, allowing platforms to match delivery tasks with the most suitable independent couriers based on proximity, availability, and route alignment (Upper Inc., 2025).
A key challenge in this domain is the Dynamic Vehicle Routing Problem (DVRP), where systems must continuously update dispatch plans in response to real-time order arrivals to minimize total service costs (PLOS One, 2024). This literature validates the need for DropCity’s core approach: utilizing advanced, dynamic algorithms (rather than static route planning) to maintain efficiency and courier engagement. Studies show that successful platforms must integrate real-time traffic data, optimize vehicle capacity, and utilize Geographic Information Systems (GIS) to create high-quality, stable delivery plans (NextBillion.ai, 2025).
1.8.3 Connectivity-Aware Architecture and Data Synchronization
For logistics and field services, the mobile application must function reliably even when network connectivity is intermittent, validating the need for an Offline-First Architecture (Desino, 2025). This architectural paradigm treats network access as an optional feature, ensuring that couriers can continue recording essential data—such as GPS pings and package status changes—without interruption (ResearchGate, 2025).
Key synchronization strategies are crucial to maintaining Data Integrity when operating across a distributed system like DropCity:
Local Storage and Queuing: Apps must employ a local database to store user actions and state, logging every change to a Data Sync Queue (Desino, 2025). This queue is essential for the Courier App to save location pings when in a dead zone.
Conflict Resolution: When the device reconnects, the synchronization logic must prevent errors. In logistics, the most common approaches rely on timestamps or sequence numbers (Last Write Wins) to resolve conflicts, ensuring that the order of actions executed offline is replicated consistently on the server (Intellisoft, 2024).
Use Case Validation: Research specifically identifies Logistics & Transportation as a primary beneficiary of offline-first design, noting that delivery drivers frequently pass through zones with weak networks and require systems that support uninterrupted data capture (IBM, 2024).
1.9 Core Technical Modules 
1.9.1 Connectivity-Aware Tracking Module 
To ensure reliable tracking despite potentially intermittent mobile network coverage, the courier mobile application will implement a Connectivity-Aware Tracking Module using local caching and asynchronous data synchronization. 
1.9.2 Offline Caching and Data Integrity 
The module relies on the native offline persistence of the cloud database (e.g., Firebase Firestore). 
• Location Data Queue: All real-time GPS location pings and timestamps are written immediately to a local data queue on the device’s persistent storage. This prevents the loss of location history in dead zones. 
• Status and Transaction Caching: Critical user interactions (accepting job, “Picked Up,” “Delivered”) are also written first to the local cache, providing the courier with instant UI feedback.
1.9.3 Asynchronous Re-synchronization Logic 
The synchronization process is background-driven and triggered by a change in network status. 
1. Connection Loss: The module operates locally, continuing to populate the data queue while displaying a ”Offline Mode” indicator. 
2. Connection Re-established: The module automatically initiates a background process to upload all buffered data (location pings and transaction statuses) to the central Firestore database. 
3. Data Integrity: Timestamps and document sequencing are used to ensure the data is merged into the cloud database in the correct temporal order, maintaining an accurate client tracking history.

1.10 Evaluation and Success Criteria 
The system’s success will be quantitatively and qualitatively evaluated during a four-week pilot study involving 10-15 couriers and 50-70 clients. 
1.10.1 Success Criteria (Quantitative) 
The project will be deemed successful if the following performance benchmarks are met: 
• Match Rate (Objective 5): A minimum of 70% of all delivery requests during the pilot period are successfully matched with a courier. 
• Data Reliability (Objective 3): The Connectivity-Aware Module must achieve a 99.9% data integrity rate, meaning less than 0.1% of buffered location pings are lost or mis-sequenced upon synchronization. 
1.10.2 Evaluation Methodology (Qualitative) 
• User Satisfaction (Objective 5): User satisfaction surveys (clients and couriers) will be distributed post-delivery, targeting a minimum 80% satisfaction rate on usability, tracking transparency, and reliability. 
• Route Efficiency Validation: The actual route efficiency will be validated by comparing the algorithm’s predicted detour against the GPS data of the route actually traveled. This comparison will assess the accuracy of the Dtravel function. 
• System Scalability: The backend will be stress-tested to handle 100 concurrent matching requests per minute without experiencing a service degradation time exceeding 1 second. 

Chapter 2: Literature Review and System Specification
2.1 Literature Review
This section identifies key research fields relevant to the DropCity platform, demonstrating that the project is grounded in established academic and industry concepts. The system uniquely integrates advanced routing algorithms, decentralized logistics models, and resilient mobile computing to solve local supply chain inefficiencies.
2.1.1 Crowdsourced and Peer-to-Peer (P2P) Logistics
The project leverages the concept of Crowdsourced Logistics (CSL), utilizing a decentralized network of independent couriers who fulfill deliveries opportunistically. Research confirms that CSL addresses the complexity of urban last-mile delivery by utilizing existing infrastructure and personal travel plans (Wang, 2020). Studies emphasize that the success of P2P models hinges on incentivizing drivers and ensuring the efficiency of the matching process, particularly when utilizing local transport networks (Dlamini, 2019). DropCity aligns with this trend by treating every commuter as a potential transporter, maximizing resource utilization within the city grid.
2.1.2 Geospatial Optimization and Route-Alignment
Route matching in this context is a specialized case of the Vehicle Routing Problem (VRP), complicated by the need for dynamic, real-time matching rather than static, pre-planned routes (Makoni, 2018). The core innovation, the Route-Alignment algorithm, is grounded in operational research focused on minimizing the marginal distance and time deviation required to service a delivery along an existing trip (Mupinga, 2020). This contrasts sharply with traditional algorithms that seek the absolute shortest path.
Geospatial data structures, such as R-trees or Quadtrees, are theoretically required to perform the rapid spatial queries needed to check the Route Alignment Condition against millions of data points in milliseconds (Ncube, 2022). This initial geometric filter determines the feasibility of a match.
Furthermore, the operational success of this matching process must incorporate incentive design; recent studies confirm that the courier’s decision to accept a job relies on balancing the monetary reward against the inconvenience and risk of the task. Therefore, the Final Score formula used for ranking will combine Route Alignment proximity and the Courier Rating, ensuring efficiency is balanced with service quality (Chen & Zhang, 2023; Li & Wei, 2023).
2.1.3 Connectivity-Aware and Offline-First Architecture
The project’s Connectivity-Aware Tracking Module is directly informed by research on Offline-First Mobile Architecture. This paradigm is essential for building resilient systems in environments with intermittent network access (Zvirikufa, 2023). Studies on logistics and field service applications validate the use of local data caching and an asynchronous synchronization queue to prevent data loss (Dlamini, 2019). The approach ensures that critical events are logged locally and merged with the central database upon connection restoration, maintaining data integrity and building user trust (Romer & Singh, 2022). This focus on resilient data synchronization is critical for operational success in developing regions.
2.1.4 Pre-existing Solutions and Market Gap Analysis
While local competitors like inDrive and established courier services offer delivery solutions, they fall into two distinct categories: dedicated fleet services (expensive) or traditional on-demand taxi services (relying on immediate proximity). The key market gap is the absence of a solution focusing on Opportunistic Logistics—the core concept of matching a client to a driver who is already committed to travelling to a similar destination, resulting in maximum efficiency and cost savings. DropCity's Route-Matching Algorithm is specifically designed to fill this niche, which currently lacks a technically mature platform. This approach is validated by literature that highlights the necessity of digitalizing the informal economy in Southern Africa to capture last-mile efficiency that traditional models ignore (Ndlovu & Moyo, 2021). The platform, therefore, creates a novelty by combining advanced geospatial filtering with necessary digital inclusion measures.
2.2 Research Methodology
2.2.1 Life Cycle Model
The project will utilize the Agile/Scrum Model. This choice is mandatory due to the project's complexity and the novel algorithmic component, which requires constant iteration and refinement.
Iterative Development: The system will be built in 2-week Sprints, allowing for the continuous testing and refinement of core components.
Algorithm Refinement: The initial matching logic will be tested early (Week 7-8). If the Route-Alignment metric fails to meet the threshold, the sprint backlog allows for immediate revision of the ranking weights (α, β).
User Feedback Integration: The Pilot Study data will feed directly back into subsequent sprints to improve the UI/UX and fix critical bugs.
2.2.2 Data Gathering Methods
Data gathering will be continuous and divided into three stages:
Interviews (Qualitative): Semi-structured interviews will be conducted with existing informal transporters and frequent parcel senders. This will gather qualitative data on pricing tolerance, ideal detour time limits, trust issues, and essential workflow needs for both the Courier App and Client App interfaces.
Observation (Qualitative/Process Mapping): The researcher will observe the current business processes of local informal dispatch points (e.g., bus stops, taxi ranks) to accurately map the manual process of accepting and managing parcels. This directly informs the workflow design of the Courier App.
Pilot Study Data (Quantitative/System Metrics): This is the primary data source for evaluation. The production system will automatically log the required data, including:
Actual Courier Routes (via GPS log).
Calculated vs. Actual Time.
User Rating Scores and Match Acceptance/Rejection Rates.
Connectivity Disruption Logs (time and location data from the Connectivity-Aware Module).

2.4 Functional Requirements (FR) and Non-Functional Requirements (NFR)
Functional Requirements (FR)
The system shall perform the following core functions:
The system shall allow a client to securely register and submit a new delivery request via the Client App.
The system shall execute the Route-Matching Algorithm to calculate the length of route and present the top 5 job matches to available courier(s) via push notification.
The system shall allow the courier to accept or reject an assigned job via the Courier App.
The Courier App shall automatically log GPS coordinates to local storage when network connectivity is lost.
The system shall automatically synchronize buffered location data to the server when network connectivity is restored (Connectivity-Aware Module).
The system shall proactively display a transparent alert to the client if the courier enters a known low-connectivity zone, explaining potential tracking delays.
The system shall allow clients to submit a numerical rating (1-5 stars) for a courier upon job completion.
The Admin Web App shall display real-time analytics, including a map of confirmed low-connectivity areas and a list of frequently used routes.
Two step confirmation of delivery, both courier and client have to confirm the package has been successfully delivered to update status to delivered.
Non-Functional Requirements (NFR)
The system shall possess the following qualities:
Performance (Matching):
Requirement: The Route-Matching Algorithm must return a ranked list of couriers to the Client App within 5 seconds of the request submission.
Fit Criterion: 95% of matching requests shall be processed in under 5 seconds.
Reliability (Data Integrity):
Requirement: The system must ensure that no location data is lost during periods of network disconnection.
Fit Criterion: The Data Integrity Rate (percentage of buffered pings successfully synchronized) shall be  99.9%.
Security (Confidentiality):
Requirement: The system must encrypt all user passwords and parcel information during transmission and storage.
Fit Criterion: All communication between the mobile apps and the backend shall enforce mandatory HTTPS/SSL/TLS encryption.

3.7 Process Design (Pre- and Post-conditions)
This section formally validates the two most crucial operational scenarios by detailing the precise conditions required for success.
3.7.1 Scenario 1: Courier Accepts Assigned Job (Most Crucial Operational Scenario)
Operation: Courier::acceptJob (jobID, courierID)
Pre-Conditions
Job Existence: A valid Job object with the status COURIER\_ASSIGNED must exist in the database. 
Courier Identity: The courierID initiating the acceptJob operation must match the courierID currently assigned to the Job. 
Route Availability: The Courier's corresponding Route must still be isActive. 
Capacity Check:The Courier's current remaining capacity must be greater than or equal to the Parcel size/weight associated with the Job.
Post-Conditions 
Job Status Update: The status of the Job object will be updated from COURIER\_ASSIGNED to ACCEPTED.
Acceptance Timestamp: The Job::acceptedAt attribute will be populated with the current server timestamp.
Courier Status Change: The status of the Courier object will be updated from AVAILABLE to BUSY (or ACTIVE\_ON\_JOB).
Client Notification: A real-time notification will be triggered, confirming the Client has a confirmed courier for their Order.
Tracking Initiation: The TrackingService will automatically begin logging the Courier App's GPS pings into the appropriate Job::locationHistory.
Parcel Link: The Job object will be permanently linked to the Order and Parcel records. 


3.7.2 Scenario 2: Logging and Synchronizing Offline Location Data (Connectivity-Aware Module)
Operation: TrackingService::synchronizeLocationBuffer (jobID, dataBatch)
Pre-Conditions
Job Active: A valid Job object must exist in Firestore and must have a status of 'ACCEPTED' or 'DELIVERING'. 
Connectivity Restored: The Courier App's ConnectivityModule must detect a functional network connection.
Local Data: The local device database (SQLite) must contain one or more unsynced LocationPing records for the active jobID. 
Data Integrity: Each ping record must contain a valid, sequentially increasing sequenceNumber and timestamp. 

Post-Conditions
Data Persistence (FR5): The entire batch of location records will be atomically merged into the Job::locationHistory array in Firestore.
Integrity Validation: The TrackingService will update the Job::lastReportedAt timestamp to reflect the latest ping in the batch, ensuring the data was applied in the correct temporal order.
Buffer Clearance (FR4): The local database on the Courier App will be completely cleared of the successfully uploaded location pings.
Connectivity Map Update: The backend will log the location and duration of the network outage to the low\_connectivity\_zones table, updating the overall connectivity map.
Client Update: The Client App will immediately display the full, recovered tracking path.
